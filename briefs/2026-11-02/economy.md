The macro stress score rose to +0.14 from +0.01 but is still deep in Calm territory, and the macro buffer slipped only to +0.49 from +0.50, so the economy remains the dashboard's anchor even though the reading edged the wrong way this week. The move came from growth, not inflation: the real economy score fell further below zero, to -0.19 from -0.01, because households are being squeezed even as the headline economy runs hot, with [real disposable income down 0.3% in September and inflation-adjusted spending flat](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026), while the [economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, led by investment and consumer spending](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026). That is the split the previous brief warned about, and jobs are still not the problem, with [initial jobless claims at 194,000](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf), but income is not keeping up with prices, and the GDPNow input to the score also eased to +0.57 from +0.72. Inflation direction held at +0.00 with core consumer prices still at 2.60% a year on the dashboard, but the Fed's preferred gauge ran warmer, with [prices up 0.5% in September and 3.6% on the year, driven by a 9.2% jump in gasoline and other energy goods, and core prices up 0.3% on the month and 3.1% on the year, in line with forecasts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/pce-inflation-report-september-2026-.html). So the previous brief's trigger, a third straight hot core print, did not clearly fire, because core matched expectations rather than surprising, though the [Fed still said inflation "remains elevated" and two officials voted to raise rates](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm). With [Brent at $117.64, a six-month high](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-jumps-2-settles-6-month-highs-fears-escalation-iran), energy keeps feeding prices, and the stress score stays low only because the framework needs weaker growth and faster inflation together, and so far it sees only the first. Friday's October jobs report is the next test, with [forecasters expecting 55,000 jobs and unemployment at 4.2% after just 29,000 jobs in September](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html): a miss would push the real economy score lower, and a hotter core inflation reading on top of that is the combination that would start eroding the buffer.
