Market momentum improved from -1.07 to -0.77 this week, but it is still well below zero, so markets are less stressed rather than healthy, and the gain came in a week when the [S&P 500 lost 1.82% on Friday alone to 7,580.07 as results from Intel and Amazon revived fears about the cost of AI](https://www.streetinsider.com/Market+Check/Wall+Street+closes+sharply+lower+as+AI+cash+burn+fears+spark+tech%C2%A0selloff/27260146.html). Breadth did most of the work again, jumping from -0.54 to -0.07, close to neutral, because the selling stayed inside technology: [most S&P 500 sectors rose on the week as investors moved into health care and consumer staples](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html), and [on the day Alphabet fell about 12.6%, the Russell 2000 index of smaller companies rose 1%](https://www.openbookanalytics.com/news/insights/us-stock-market-weekly-recap-2026-10-30). The damage is concentrated in chips, where the [semiconductor index fell 7.2% on Friday and ended October down more than 15%](https://www.streetinsider.com/Market+Check/Wall+Street+closes+sharply+lower+as+AI+cash+burn+fears+spark+tech%C2%A0selloff/27260146.html), and chip stocks are now the biggest drag inside breadth. Financial conditions, the one driver that got worse last week, improved from -1.54 to -1.17 as bond volatility eased to 100.20 from 103.31 and the high-yield spread tightened to 3.26 from 3.40, while the VIX was flat at 19.42 versus 19.45. Growth impulses barely moved, from -1.14 to -1.07, with a steeper yield curve, the gap between 10-year and 2-year yields at 0.37 versus 0.31, doing most of the lifting while riskier stocks keep losing badly to low-volatility ones. Last week's watch-points resolved in mixed fashion: the [Fed held at 3.75% to 4% but two officials voted for a hike](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm), the 10-year yield stayed below 5% at 4.98 even as [Brent settled at $117.64, a six-month high, after a tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-jumps-2-settles-6-month-highs-fears-escalation-iran), and the hyperscaler test showed how investors now grade AI spending, punishing [Alphabet's roughly negative $9.1 billion of quarterly free cash flow and Amazon's negative $42.4 billion over twelve months while Microsoft, whose cash flow held up, rose](https://www.openbookanalytics.com/news/insights/us-stock-market-weekly-recap-2026-10-30). Momentum keeps improving only if the selling stays confined to AI and credit keeps calming, so the tests this week are Tuesday's midterms, after which [traders are bracing for a possible escalation in the Iran war](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-jumps-2-settles-6-month-highs-fears-escalation-iran), and Friday's jobs report, where [forecasters expect 55,000 new jobs and unemployment at 4.2%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html); selling that spreads from chips into banks, small companies and credit would undo the breadth gain.
