The macro stress score held at +0.00 for a third consecutive week — firmly Calm — and the macro buffer remains at full strength (+0.50), meaning the economy pillar is still contributing maximum tailwind to the headline posture. But the growth picture underneath that reading is materially softer than it was seven days ago. [September nonfarm payrolls printed just 29,000](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/september-2026-jobs-report-payrolls-123334753.html) against an 84,000 forecast, the unemployment rate ticked to 4.2%, July was revised to an outright loss of 10,000 jobs, and [GDPNow was cut from 5.0% to 3.7%](https://investinglive.com/central-banks/atlanta-fed-gdpnow-cuts-q3-us-growth-estimate-to-3-7-from-5-0/) after an unexpectedly wide August goods trade deficit pushed net exports from -1.37 to -2.60 percentage points. The real economy score ticked to +0.05 and did not flip negative because the same "low hire, low fire" pattern that has held all year is still intact: [initial claims came in at 197,000 for the week ending September 26](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-01/us-weekly-jobless-claims-fall-layoffs-drop-in-september), near 57-year lows, and September wages grew just 0.1% on the month and 3.0% over the year — no mass layoffs, but no real hiring momentum either, with the August real spending print (+0.6% month over month from the PCE release) providing a small offsetting lift. On inflation, last week's open question is closed cleanly: the [August PCE report](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/august-2026-pce-inflation) showed core up 0.2% on the month and 3.0% year over year, both below consensus, so the inflation direction is unchanged at -0.10 and core CPI sits at 2.40%. The October 14 CPI print is what changes this picture or doesn't: another monthly core reading of 0.3% or higher would push the inflation axis toward neutral, and if the labor market keeps printing sub-50,000 payrolls and eventually drags the real economy score below zero, both axes would be moving toward stress at the same time — the only path that starts to erode the macro buffer from its current ceiling.
