Financial conditions collapsed -1.04 to -1.32 this week, the largest single-week move in that component during the current cycle, and dragged market momentum from -0.59 down to -0.80 even as growth impulses and breadth both managed gains of +0.20 each — to -0.36 and -0.74 respectively — improvements that were simply overwhelmed by the scale of the conditions deterioration. The week's forcing function was a [September payrolls print of just 29,000](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html) — less than a third of the 84,000 expected, with July and August revised down a combined 60,000 — confirming the labor market is cracking under the weight of the Fed's tightening cycle rather than simply cooling. That weak print cut October hike odds and explains why growth impulses and breadth found marginal footing: lower rate expectations briefly pulled the [10-year Treasury yield back from a 5.28% intraday high before it settled at 5.24%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/treasury-yields-bonds-nonfarm-payrolls.html), but 5.24% is still a multi-decade extreme, and with the [MOVE index at 107.29](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/bonds/26/09/62051889/move-index-bond-volatility-vix-september-2026) — roughly 35% above where it entered September — bond volatility remains the dominant drag on financial conditions. The Strait of Hormuz, last week's key watch-point, is still closed: [Iran has now set seven formal conditions for reopening](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/10/04/iran-sets-conditions-for-reopening-strait-of-hormuz-amid-global-energy-concerns/) and [talks are at an impasse](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/6021575-iran-oman-hormuz-strait-deal/), so the stagflationary supply disruption continues to compound the Fed's problem — weak demand and supply-driven inflation at the same time, with no easy policy response. The starkest divergence in the data is the VIX sitting at 15.31 while the MOVE holds at 107.29: equity markets are still priced as though conditions are benign, but a 29,000-job September is not benign, and if earnings expectations begin adjusting to the growth shortfall, that gap closes through equity selling rather than rates calming — the worse of the two paths for financial conditions and market momentum. The view turns on three catalysts in priority order: an explicit Fed pause signal that convinces the bond market the rate cycle is finished, a Hormuz resolution that lifts the stagflationary ceiling, or a rapid VIX repricing that clears the equity mispricing in one move rather than a slow grind lower.
