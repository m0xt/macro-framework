Macro stress holds at +0.00 — Calm and at the floor of the 0–10 scale — with the macro buffer at full strength (+0.50), but Friday's July jobs report delivered the most significant growth scare of the cycle and has left the real economy score barely positive at +0.04. [Nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 in July](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/EconomicResearch/2026/8/7/nonfarm-payrolls-declined-23,000-in-july) — the first outright job loss in over two years, well below a consensus expecting gains north of 80,000 — and revisions stripped 103,000 off May and June combined, meaning the labor market has been cooling for longer than the headline prints made clear. GDPNow's sub-component score of +1.79 is carrying the real economy composite above zero for now, but it reflects the [August 6 estimate of 5.8% annualized Q3 growth](https://www.fx.co/en/forex-news/3085622) and has not yet absorbed the payrolls data — the model's next scheduled update is August 14, and the jobs miss should move it lower. The inflation direction metric is flat at +0.00, and the payrolls report offers one genuine piece of relief: [average hourly earnings growth slowed to 3.2% year-over-year](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm), the softest since mid-2021, which reduces the services-side wage pressure that was the main inflation concern going into Wednesday's CPI release; the countervailing signal is [ISM Services Prices Paid at 70.3 in July](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/services-pmi-at-54-1-july-2026-ism-services-pmi-report-302843134.html) — above 70 for the fourth time in five months — with services employment simultaneously falling back into contraction at 47.4, meaning the sector is charging more while adding fewer workers. Wednesday's CPI print is now doubly consequential: a monthly core reading of 0.20% or higher would move the inflation direction metric off zero at the same moment growth is softening, putting both axes of the stress score in the wrong position simultaneously, which is the combination the framework penalizes most severely; a softer print, credibly supported by slower wage growth, would keep inflation flat and leave stress Calm while the real economy picture waits on the August 14 GDPNow revision to fully account for the employment miss.
