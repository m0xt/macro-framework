The macro stress score sits at **+0.06** — deep in the Calm band, with the full-strength macro buffer of **+0.50** intact and unchanged. That reading has barely moved in two weeks, which makes it easy to miss that the situation around it has shifted materially: the [August Consumer Price Index, released September 11](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-core-cpi-rises-more-than-forecast-bolstering-case-for-hike), showed core prices up 0.3% on the month — a tenth above forecast and the second consecutive 0.3% print — and that single data point drove Fed hike odds from roughly 56% to [above 85–90%](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxfeddecision/fed-meeting/kxfeddecision-26sep) with the decision landing tomorrow. The stress score stayed flat because the framework watches what matters for the trend: the inflation direction axis measures the change in core inflation's annual rate over six months, and that six-month delta is still **-0.10 percentage points** — meaning core Consumer Price Index year-over-year, currently at **2.40%**, is still drifting lower over any medium-term window even as each individual monthly print runs hot. On the growth side, the real economy score is at **-0.08**, essentially neutral, with [GDPNow tracking Q3 at just **+1.17%**](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow) annualized and [jobless claims steady at 206,000](https://www.usnews.com/news/business/articles/2026-09-10/us-unemployment-claims-dip-to-206-000-as-layoffs-continue-to-remain-rare) — soft growth but no break in hiring. The previous brief named the Fed decision as the dominant catalyst, and tomorrow's call now has nearly the highest urgency it could short of being a certainty: a hike into a sub-2% GDPNow reading is the most direct path to stress beginning to build, because it would simultaneously weigh on spending and income data (pushing the real economy score further negative) while the inflation direction axis has yet to confirm that monthly momentum is reversing the annual trend. What would actually move the stress score off the floor is a combination: tomorrow's hike followed by a [September Consumer Price Index print in mid-October](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm) showing a third 0.3% monthly gain — that sequence would finally start bending the six-month inflation direction upward and, if growth indicators respond to the tighter policy, could push stress into the Watch range for the first time this cycle.
