Market momentum has crossed into CASH territory, falling to -0.61 as of Monday morning — past the -0.50 line that separates CAUTION from CASH — before the [Federal Reserve has even announced its September 16 decision](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/fed-meeting-updates-and-commentary-september-2026). The previous brief warned that only 0.02 of cushion remained; what closed that gap was not a Fed hike but a collision of pressures: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a Saturday essay calling for AI companies to pull back on advancing capabilities, drawing backing from Sam Altman and Elon Musk, and the [resulting Monday chipmaker rout](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/ai-stocks-slowdown-amodei-altman.html) sent the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down nearly 6% and pushed the VIX from 15.84 to 17.75, while [the 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched 5% for the first time since 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/10-year-us-treasury-is-closing-in-on-5percent.html) before settling at 4.96%. Financial conditions fell to +0.07 from +0.20 — the VIX jump more than offset continued tightness in high-yield credit spreads, which edged in slightly to 2.65 basis points — and the one component that had been carrying the market pillar for weeks can no longer compensate for everything else pulling in the wrong direction. Growth impulses dropped to -0.69, down -0.26 on the day and -0.65 over seven days, with [copper sliding to roughly $6.28 per pound](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/copper) on rising Fed hike expectations and the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis) still logging only around six commercial transits per day against a pre-crisis norm near 85 — shipping remains a consistent drag on the growth impulse score with no near-term catalyst for improvement. Breadth is stuck at -1.20, unchanged on the day, with roughly a third of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day average; the Monday selloff concentrated in semiconductors and was broad enough across global markets to suggest the participation problem is not bottoming. The Fed meeting is now the only realistic near-term circuit breaker: [prediction markets price the September 16 hike at roughly 50–56%](https://robinhood.com/us/en/prediction-markets/economics/events/fed-rate-decision-in-september-2026-sep-16-2026/), and a hold paired with credible, forward-looking language on the inflation path is the minimum condition to stop the growth impulse and breadth slide — a hike with hawkish guidance on energy-driven inflation would deepen both, and the score has no cushion left to absorb it.
