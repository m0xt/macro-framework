The macro stress score sits at +0.01, up from +0.00 and still firmly Calm, with the macro buffer holding at +0.50: the economy remains the dashboard's anchor because growth and inflation are not getting worse at the same time. The previous brief worried about the real economy score sliding toward zero, and it has now crossed it, slipping to -0.01 from +0.02, but that reflects soft real income and spending growth more than any crack in jobs, since [initial jobless claims fell to 194,000](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf), near the lowest since the late 1960s, and the [flash business survey pointed to growth at roughly a 5% annualized pace](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/10/us-flash-pmi-signals-sustained-strong-growth-but-also-further-inflation-uplift-in-october). The weak spot is households, with [consumer sentiment at 48.0, the second-lowest reading on record](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/us-consumer-sentiment-near-record-141940567.html), as higher prices and higher interest rates squeeze purchasing power. Inflation direction held at +0.00 with core consumer prices at 2.60% a year, so the six-month trend is flat rather than rising, but the pipeline is filling: the same survey showed [selling prices rising at the sharpest rate since August 2022](https://www.pmi.spglobal.com/Public/Home/PressRelease/07d16568e63346999890940d47639cd9), and [consumers' year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7% and their five-year expectations to 3.5%](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/), both the highest since May. With [Brent at $116.72, its highest close since April](https://petra.gov.jo/en/news/oil-prices-jump-more-than-3-to-six-month-high), the energy shock behind September's hot inflation print is getting bigger, not fading. The stress score has not moved because growth is only a hair below zero and inflation direction is flat, and the framework needs one of them to move decisively, ideally both together, before it registers stress. The previous brief's trigger, a third straight hot monthly core print, gets its first test on Thursday, when [third-quarter growth and September consumer spending and prices land together, with forecasters expecting 3.2% growth and core spending prices up 0.3% on the month and 3.1% on the year](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202610237517/3q-gdp-growth-seen-higher-data-week-ahead); a hot core reading alongside a real economy score that keeps sinking below zero is the combination that would start eroding the buffer.
