Market momentum fell from -0.68 to -1.26 this week, with all three sub-components moving lower simultaneously and no offset anywhere in the dashboard. The previous brief flagged two specific risks: the gap between calm equity markets and stressed bond markets eventually closing in the wrong direction, and the fragility of the MOVE's improvement since markets had priced a Fed skip, not a stop — both resolved badly this week. [September CPI came in at 3.7% year-over-year with core at 2.6%](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/september-cpi-report-inflation-energy-costs/), above forecasts on both measures, [cementing December as a near-certain hike at every major brokerage](https://kfgo.com/2026/10/15/factbox-major-brokerages-expect-next-fed-rate-hike-in-december/) and pushing October odds back above 27%, which lifted the MOVE back to 101.10 from 98.47 and sent the VIX from 14.84 to 19.99 as equity markets finally stopped ignoring what bonds had been pricing — financial conditions fell from -0.79 to -1.38. The deeper damage is in growth impulses, which collapsed from -0.44 to -1.39, driven by an [AI credit stress event that spread through the entire tech supply chain](https://diywallst.substack.com/p/the-ai-trade-finally-cracked-now): the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell for eight consecutive sessions, [AI cloud credit buckled with CoreWeave bonds trading near 78 cents on the dollar](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/sk-hynix-samsung-softbank-stocks-asia-tech-rout-ai-funding-worries.html) and default-swap markets pricing more than a 60% five-year default probability, hitting the credit spread, sector-rotation, and shipping inputs that feed growth impulses from multiple directions at once. Breadth fell further from -0.81 to -1.02 as [rotation went defensive into energy and staples](https://premarketbrief.substack.com/p/tech-sold-off-and-money-rotated-into) — a narrowing of leadership, not a broadening, which is the opposite of what would be needed to lift that sub-component. [The Strait of Hormuz remains closed on Day 231](https://straits.live/briefs/2026-10-19), attack frequency running nearly double the monthly average and talks at an impasse, which means the energy cost surge embedded in September CPI is not a one-off — the Fed is still trying to fight supply-driven inflation with a rate tool designed for demand, and there is no clean path out of that. Market momentum improves only if the October 27–28 FOMC meeting surprises dovishly, AI credit stress stops short of spreading into broader high-yield, or Hormuz reopens; none of those conditions are in view.
