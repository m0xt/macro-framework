The macro stress score sits at **+0.04** — Calm, and effectively unchanged overnight — while the macro buffer holds at full strength (**+0.50**), meaning the economy pillar continues to give the headline allocation posture its maximum tailwind. This week's data gave a partial answer to the previous brief's central question: whether private-sector hiring softness would spread into spending and income. It has begun to. [Consumer spending](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/26/economy/pce-consumer-spending-inflation-july) was flat in July after a 0.4% gain in June, retail sales fell 0.6% — the steepest drop since May 2025 — and [University of Michigan consumer sentiment](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/business/2026/08/14/u-mich-consumer-sentiment-august-2026) dropped 8% in August amid rising energy and food costs. The hiring picture itself is split: the [Bureau of Labor Statistics August payrolls report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm) showed 162,000 jobs — a significant beat against the roughly 53,000 consensus, with June–July revised up a combined 55,000 — while [ADP's private-sector count](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2026-09-02-ADP-National-Employment-Report-Private-Sector-Employment-Increased-by-38,000-Jobs-in-August) was far softer at 38,000, the weakest since January, with manufacturing and professional services shedding jobs even as education and health hired. The real economy score at **-0.06** captures this cross-current accurately: [GDPNow is still tracking Q3 at 4.7% annualized](https://www.fx.co/en/forex-news/3140848) and the services sector remains in solid expansion, so the composite stays barely negative rather than sliding. Inflation is offering no pressure from either side: core Consumer Price Index is anchored at 2.50% year-over-year, the direction reading is **+0.00**, and average hourly earnings growth of 3.1% year-over-year provides no wage-push signal to worry about. The [August Consumer Price Index on September 11](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) remains the clearest near-term catalyst — re-acceleration in core prices would begin eroding the macro buffer, while continued spending softness without an inflation uptick would leave the stress score barely stirring inside Calm.
