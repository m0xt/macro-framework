The macro stress score holds at **+0.04** — Calm — with both underlying axes unchanged on the week and the macro buffer still at full strength (**+0.50**). The previous brief's open question — whether Thursday's August Consumer Price Index would show consumer-facing pass-through from elevated services-sector cost pressure — remains exactly that: the [Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the August print on September 11](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/), and the setup heading into it has if anything gotten sharper, with the [Minneapolis Fed now formally noting](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/initially-delayed-the-pass-through-of-tariffs-to-consumer-prices-has-arrived) that tariff cost pass-through has reached consumers and is estimated to be adding 0.2 to 0.4 percentage points to core prices. On the real economy side, the picture is steadier than the **-0.05** real economy score implies: that fractionally negative reading sits against a backdrop of [Q3 GDPNow running at 4.7% annualized as of September 3](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow), with tomorrow's scheduled update the next read on whether August's strong payrolls report has pushed the nowcast higher. The inflation direction axis reads **+0.00** with core Consumer Price Index still at 2.50% year-over-year; that gauge measures the six-month change in the annual rate and moves slowly, but [August ISM Services Prices Paid at 72.6](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/services-pmi-at-55-4-august-2026-ism-services-pmi-report-302868046.html) — its highest since 2022 — alongside the Minneapolis Fed's tariff evidence means the pressure feeding that gauge has not eased between last week and now. Thursday's Consumer Price Index is the single most important near-term data point for this pillar: a monthly core reading at or above 0.3% would be the first concrete evidence that upstream cost pressure is moving into consumer prices at pace, beginning to tilt the six-month change positive and starting to erode the macro buffer; a 0.4% print would accelerate that path materially.
