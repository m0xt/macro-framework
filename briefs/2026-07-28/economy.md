The macro stress score holds at +0.48, unchanged in character from last week and firmly in the Calm range, with the macro buffer at +0.48 — near-full strength and still supporting the headline posture rather than eroding it. The prior brief's open question is now closed, and the answer went the wrong way: [June durable goods orders rose just 0.3% against a +1.6% consensus](https://www.rttnews.com/story.aspx?Id=3670812), and [Atlanta Fed GDPNow responded on July 27 by trimming its Q2 estimate to 1.6%](https://x.com/AtlantaFed/status/2081742788790473208) — investment growth was cut, not lifted, moving in the opposite direction from what the durable goods rebound was supposed to deliver. The labor market is pushing back hard against that softness: [initial claims fell to 187,000 for the week ending July 18](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2026/jul/24/us-filings-for-unemployment-aid-fall-to-187000/), the lowest since 1969 and roughly 23,000 below consensus, which kept the real economy score at -0.64 (+0.04 on the week) from deteriorating further. Inflation still adds no stress: core CPI sits at 2.60% year-over-year, the direction axis is flat at +0.00 for a second consecutive week, and there is still no evidence that tariff costs are flowing through to consumer prices. The [Bureau of Economic Analysis Q2 advance GDP estimate on Wednesday](https://www.bea.gov/taxonomy/term/451) is the immediate test — [professional forecasters hold at 2.1% annualized](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/spf-q2-2026) while GDPNow is tracking half a point lower at 1.6%, and a result near or below that lower bound would push the real economy score further from neutral and confirm the soft-growth read rather than resolving it. The scenario that flips the framework remains a specific double-hit: a sub-1.5% GDP print followed by [July CPI near 3.0% year-over-year on August 12](https://www.morningstar.com/economy/july-cpi-report-forecasts-show-continued-tariff-driven-inflation) as tariff goods begin to flow through is what turns the inflation direction axis positive and starts testing the macro buffer in earnest.
