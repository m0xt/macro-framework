Market momentum has dropped to +0.13, crossing below the +0.25 line that separates LONG from CAUTION — the previous brief said this was the risk, and the week delivered it. The shift was not driven by breadth, which barely moved, losing just -0.02 to hold at +0.60, though the seven-day drop of -0.44 on this component is a reminder that participation has been quietly narrowing all week. What pushed market momentum lower was growth impulses, down another -0.23 to -0.45 and now off -0.53 over the past seven days, as the [Houthi-driven oil and shipping shock](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/07/23/least-one-saudi-oil-tanker-is-attacked-red-sea-war-risks-widen/) — crude above $100 for most of the week — kept worsening the shipping and yield-curve inputs that feed that component. Financial conditions also fell, sliding -0.16 to +0.24, though the underlying picture is less alarming: the VIX ticked slightly lower to 18.58 and high-yield credit spreads sit at 2.77, barely wider than yesterday and well below any level historically associated with real stress, so this is real-economy turbulence rather than a credit or volatility event. One partial offset emerged Friday: oil pulled back from its highs [after reports of Pakistan-backed U.S.-Iran diplomatic talks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/oil-price-trump-hormuz-red-sea-iran-war.html), which, if they develop, could ease the shipping and commodity pressure that has been battering growth impulses all week. The read stays in CAUTION ahead of [Microsoft and Meta on July 29](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/meta-microsoft-face-renewed-unease-over-ai-spending-us-earnings-week-ahead/): after back-to-back mega-cap AI misses from Alphabet and Tesla, the market will reward any credible sign that AI capital spending is generating real revenue — Azure cloud growth or Meta ad strength could push market momentum back toward the LONG line, while another round of capex-heavy disappointment pulls it further from it.
