Market momentum bounced from -0.66 to -0.49, crossing back above the -0.50 floor where CASH begins — but the one-day improvement is almost entirely a volatility story. The VIX fell from 17.84 to 15.84 as Thursday's CPI shock was absorbed, lifting financial conditions from +0.00 to +0.20 and accounting for nearly all of the +0.17 day-over-day gain; high yield credit spreads held at a historically tight 2.70 and bond market volatility at 82.21 stayed contained, making financial conditions the lone positive component in the dashboard. Breadth edged up +0.27 to -1.24, but the seven-day change of -0.95 tells the real story: seven sessions of outflows from economically sensitive sectors have left almost no broad participation in the rally, and one day's partial improvement does not reverse that. Growth impulses recovered just +0.04 to -0.42, with copper near record highs on [supply disruptions in Chile and Peru](https://www.riotimesonline.com/copper-markets-latam-wednesday-september-9-2026/) providing a small offset, while Hormuz shipping remains severely disrupted — [tit-for-tat United States and Iran tanker strikes through September 8](https://www.unitedagainstnucleariran.com/analysis/iran-shipping-update-september-8-2026) ended the brief freight recovery and waterway volumes stay far below normal. At -0.49, market momentum sits one tick above the CASH line and weekend gap risk is real — any fresh geopolitical headline before Monday could flip it back over. The [Fed on September 15–16](https://cambridgecurrencies.com/next-federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision/) is the decisive event: a hold with softer forward guidance is the minimum needed for breadth to stabilize and financial conditions to keep building, while a [25-basis-point hike](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/learning-and-insights/article/september-2026-rate-hike-now-expected-amid-energy-shocks) with hawkish language would almost certainly push market momentum back through -0.50 and restore the CASH posture.
