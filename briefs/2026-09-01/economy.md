The macro stress score holds at +0.06 — solidly Calm — and the macro buffer remains fully intact at +0.50, but the key internal shift this week is that the real economy score has crossed below zero for the first time this cycle, reaching -0.08. The primary driver was the [July PCE report released August 26](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-july-2026), which showed real personal spending essentially flat — ending ten consecutive months of consumption growth — while the [Q2 GDP second estimate](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-second-estimate-and-corporate-profits-2nd-quarter-2026) confirmed that activity slowed to 1.5% annualized last quarter, and GDPNow for the current quarter pulled back to +1.24 from +1.79 two weeks ago. The real economy score is already recovering at the margin — the 7-day change is +0.07 — and [initial jobless claims fell to 203,000](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/u-weekly-jobless-claims-fall-124023010.html) for the most recent week, holding near historic lows, so the labor market is not yet sending a clearly negative signal despite the softer spending data. Inflation direction stays at +0.00, with core CPI anchored at 2.50% year-over-year; however, [the same PCE release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-july-2026) showed core PCE running at 3.3% year-over-year — well above the CPI equivalent — likely reflecting tariff passthrough in goods categories that the PCE basket weights more heavily, a gap [pushing odds of a September Fed rate hike toward roughly 40%](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/325655/20260826/pce-holds-37-real-spending-stalls-september-rate-hike-odds-rise-four-ten.htm). Because the framework's stress score runs off core CPI, that PCE-CPI divergence is not contributing to stress today — but if tariff effects migrate into CPI services and shelter reads over coming months, the inflation axis would turn positive and begin compounding the already-softened growth signal. August payrolls on September 4 is the single number that could materially change this picture: [consensus is around 55,000](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-29/us-jobs-report-seen-backing-warsh-view-of-labor-market) after July's -23,000 decline, and a second consecutive miss would push the real economy score deeper into negative territory and deliver the first genuine move toward Watch territory.
