The previous brief asked whether markets could recover before the September 11 Consumer Price Index — they have, but only barely. Market momentum crossed from -0.08 to +0.05, clearing zero a week ahead of that data trigger, but the move is driven entirely by financial conditions at +0.69 as bond markets price in a September hold; growth impulses at -0.24 and breadth at -0.29 are both still negative, making this a narrow crossing rather than a broad one. The economy is providing everything the framework needs underneath: the [August ISM Services PMI at 55.4](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/services-pmi-at-55-4-august-2026-ism-services-pmi-report-302868046.html) confirmed domestic spending is intact despite the 22,000-job payrolls miss, [GDPNow holds Q3 at 4.7%](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow), stress is essentially flat at +0.04, and the macro buffer holds at full strength at +0.50. Together those two pillars moved the headline allocation posture from -0.33 to -0.20 — still CAUTION at 75% exposure, but with meaningful distance from the -0.50 CASH boundary for the first time this week. What hasn't shifted is the character of the market recovery: cyclicals haven't reclaimed leadership, the seven-day breadth reading of -0.35 reflects the real damage done this week, and [the Strait of Hormuz on Day 188](https://straits.live/) with fresh military escalation means the supply shock remains live, keeping growth impulses from moving sustainably positive. The framework sits at mid-CAUTION with the economy holding the floor and markets recovering on rate-hold sentiment, not improving fundamentals — the [August Consumer Price Index on September 11](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) remains the only near-term data point that changes the picture, where a clean disinflation read gives breadth room to rebuild and opens a path toward LONG, and a sticky print tests whether the financial conditions gain survives and likely pulls the posture back toward -0.33 or below.
