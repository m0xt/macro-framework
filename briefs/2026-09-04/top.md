The payrolls print the previous brief flagged as decisive came in at [22,000](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/non-farm-payrolls/news/483228) — weak enough to confirm the Hormuz shock has reached the labor market, but not a second consecutive outright loss, and that distinction mattered. [Governor Waller's statement](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/fed-governor-waller-indicates-he-will-support-holding-rates-steady-at-september-meeting.html) that he would back a September hold if disinflation continues sent hike odds below 55%, brought the VIX to 14.32, and lifted market momentum from -0.23 to -0.08, carrying the headline allocation posture from -0.49 to -0.33 — still CAUTION at 75% exposure, but with real distance from the CASH floor for the first time in days. The economy is providing the structural support underneath: the macro buffer is at full strength at +0.50, stress is anchored at +0.05, and [GDPNow tracking Q3 at roughly 4.8% annualized](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow) shows the real economy has not yet absorbed the full force of the Hormuz closure — reassuring for now, but also a signal the worst of the supply shock may still be in transit. Markets are recovering on sentiment rather than on a genuine reversal of conditions: breadth is still negative at -0.51 despite today's partial bounce, growth impulses remain at -0.37 with the [Strait on Day 187](https://straits.live/) and copper at multi-month lows under a strong dollar, and the seven-day market momentum reading of -0.33 captures what this week actually cost even though the daily number has turned. That divergence — solid macro floor, markets still cautious — is exactly what mid-CAUTION should look like. The [August Consumer Price Index on September 11](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) is now the one data point that can move the posture in either direction: a clean disinflation print keeps Waller's hold case intact, gives breadth room to rebuild, and could push market momentum positive for the first time in over a week, while a sticky reading reopens the September hike debate, tests whether today's financial conditions gains survive, and would likely push the posture back through the floor into CASH.
