The headline allocation posture climbed from -0.60 to -0.49, crossing back out of CASH into CAUTION at 75% exposure — but the margin is a single basis point and the underlying supply shock hasn't moved. [New York Fed President Williams's September 2 remarks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/02/new-york-feds-williams-says-yield-surge-due-to-strong-economic-prospects.html) that a rate hike is not inevitable were enough to push nine of eleven sectors higher, lifting breadth from -1.01 to -0.69 and carrying market momentum from -0.35 to -0.23. The [Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed](https://straits.live/), growth impulses are stuck at -0.44, and the defensive sector rotation has not reversed — meaning the posture recovered on sentiment, not on any change to the conditions that triggered the drop to CASH. The economy is providing maximum support: the macro buffer is at full strength at +0.50, stress is anchored near the floor at +0.04, and [GDPNow tracking Q3 at 4.8%](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow) shows the real economy has not yet absorbed the Hormuz disruption — which is reassuring for now but also means the worst of the supply shock may not have landed in the data yet. With [high-yield spreads at 2.65%](https://convextrade.com/metrics/bamlh0a0hym2) and the VIX at 15.20, credit is still holding and keeping stress pinned, but the posture is one bad session away from falling back through the floor. Tomorrow's August payrolls report settles the immediate question the prior brief left open: a rebound from July's -23,000 miss would lift the real economy score, reinforce the macro buffer's durability, and give CAUTION firmer footing; a second consecutive decline extends the growth-shock narrative, deepens the pressure on growth impulses, and would likely move the posture back to CASH on its own.
