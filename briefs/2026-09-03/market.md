Market momentum recovered to -0.23 (up +0.11 on the day), but the seven-day read of -0.49 tells the truer story: breadth is -0.95 lower than a week ago and still sits at -0.69, meaning fewer than half of S&P 500 members are trading above their 50-day averages even after yesterday's relief. That bounce came entirely from [New York Fed President Williams pushing back on September 2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/02/new-york-feds-williams-says-yield-surge-due-to-strong-economic-prospects.html) against the idea that a rate hike is inevitable, attributing the move in Treasury yields to AI and data-center investment demand rather than an inflation emergency — nine of eleven sectors finished higher that day with roughly 60% of S&P 500 members advancing, which is what moved the breadth needle from -1.01 to -0.69. But Williams's remarks did not change the underlying reason breadth broke down in the first place: growth impulses remain stuck at -0.44 (up just +0.01) because [the Strait of Hormuz is still effectively closed](https://straits.live/), the shipping disruption is still in place, and the defensive sector rotation that preceded the selloff has not unwound. Financial conditions at +0.42 remain the only component in positive territory, and even that improvement is narrow: the VIX fell from 16.34 to 15.20, doing most of the work, while bond volatility ticked up to 79.71 and [high-yield spreads widened slightly to 2.65%](https://convextrade.com/metrics/bamlh0a0hym2) — credit is holding, not improving. Tomorrow's jobs report is the event the previous brief flagged, and it arrives with unusually wide dispersion: [forecasts range from a +80,000 rebound to a -25,000 miss](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/economy/jobs-report-august-2026-what-to-expect), following July's unexpected -23,000 print, which means the market genuinely does not know whether Warsh's inflation case or the Hormuz growth-shock read wins. A strong print hands Warsh the data he needs for September and puts fresh pressure on breadth; a miss embeds the growth-shock narrative more deeply into growth impulses and tests whether financial conditions can keep market momentum from breaking lower on its own.
