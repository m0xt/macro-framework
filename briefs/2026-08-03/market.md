Growth impulses at -0.26, up +0.19 on the week, have been recovering mainly because the yield curve is doing the lifting: the ten-year Treasury sits 45 basis points above the two-year, and [with markets now pricing a September Federal Reserve cut following the weak July jobs print](https://www.ubp.com/en/news-insights/newsroom/ubp-weekly-view-soft-us-labour-data-raise-fed-rate-cut-expectations), the short end has more room to fall while the long end holds, which would steepen the curve further in growth impulses' favor — the oil drop is now reinforcing the same direction from a second angle. Breadth is still the component not keeping up: at +0.21, down -0.38 on the week, it is actually falling even as [the equal-weight S&P 500 has been quietly outperforming its cap-weighted counterpart](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/equal-weight-p-500-quietly-133117336.html) and [sector rotation away from mega-cap technology toward energy, industrials, and consumer names accelerated into August](https://www.interactivecrypto.com/spy-edges-higher-amid-sector-rotation-and-divergent-tech-earnings-on-august-02-2026-aug-2026) — the gap between what that rotation implies about participation and what the dashboard's breadth score is reflecting is the central tension right now. The regime stays in CAUTION; the path to LONG now has two clearer catalysts than it did seven days ago — the Iran deal hardening below $80 oil (which would push growth impulses meaningfully toward zero) and breadth catching up to the rotation signal that equal-weight performance is already sending.
