The macro stress score eased to +0.07 from +0.14 and remains deep in Calm territory, while the macro buffer edged up to +0.50 from +0.49, so the economy pillar still anchors the dashboard even after a noisy week of politics and markets. The improvement came from growth, not inflation: the real economy score rose to -0.09 from -0.19 after [employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far above forecasts near 55,000 to 65,000, unemployment fell to 4.1%, and the prior two months were revised up by a combined 84,000 jobs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/jobs-report-october-2026.html), while Atlanta Fed GDPNow also lifted to about 3.44% from 3.22%. That answers last week's jobs watch-point clearly on the strong side, and it eases the household squeeze story without erasing it, because income remains the soft leg inside the real economy score even as hiring and output look firm. Inflation direction held at +0.00 with core consumer prices still at 2.60% a year on the dashboard, and the [steep drop in oil after the midterms, with Brent off about 17% on the week](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/oil-falls-as-trump-says-new-iran-attacks-unlikely.html), cuts the near-term energy pressure that had been feeding prices. So the prior trigger, weaker growth plus hotter inflation together, moved further away this week, not closer, even as [markets still price roughly a 78% chance of a December Fed hike](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/traders-raise-bets-fed-rate-134402668.html) after the jobs beat. The stress score stays Calm unless growth turns down again or core inflation reaccelerates, so the next tests are [Tuesday's October consumer price report](https://www.morningstar.com/economy/october-cpi-forecasts-show-inflation-softening-december-rate-hike-still-looks-likely) and whether oil's peace premium sticks if Iran talks stall; a soft core print would keep the buffer near full strength, while a hot one on top of strong wages would start to rebuild stress.
