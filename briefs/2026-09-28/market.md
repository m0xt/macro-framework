The previous brief's watch-point — whether breadth would sustain its tentative recovery — got answered, but the more important development this week happened somewhere else entirely. Financial conditions collapsed -0.64 to -0.27, fully reversing the post-Fed relief that had led the recovery a week ago, and it pulled market momentum from -0.32 down to -0.59: the [MOVE index surged roughly 30% in a single week](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/treasury-volatility-set-for-biggest-jump-in-year-as-bonds-churn) — its largest weekly jump since Liberation Day in April 2025 — as the [10-year Treasury yield pushed to 5.18%](https://invezz.com/news/2026/09/25/10-year-treasury-yield-holds-near-5-17-as-global-bond-selloff-continues/), its highest level since 2007, while the 30-year broke 5.50% for the first time since 2004. The September 16 Fed hike gave the bond selloff its legs: a [162,000 August jobs print](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm) and a composite PMI at 58.4 — the strongest since July 2021 — made the Fed's higher-for-longer signal credible, so markets spent the week repricing the entire forward curve upward. Growth impulses fell -0.43 to -0.56 as copper pulled back to roughly $6.55 per pound from its record high and the [Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed](https://straits.live/briefs/2026-09-26), with just one vessel transiting on September 20 against a normal 85 per day — this is stagflationary supply disruption that compounds the Fed's problem rather than signaling any genuine demand collapse. Breadth did tick up +0.25 to -0.94, which addresses last week's question only on the surface: the gain traces back to the September 21 AI surge that briefly lifted Intel, Meta, and AMD by 9% or more, not to real broadening — the S&P MidCap 400 and SmallCap 600 are still down roughly 7% from their August highs, and [only a fraction of stocks have reclaimed their 50-day trend lines](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/23/stocks-breadth-signals-sp). The sharpest near-term signal is the gap between equity and bond volatility: the VIX held at 14.87 even as rate vol surged, meaning [equity markets have not yet priced the rates dislocation](https://www.home.saxo/en-mena/content/articles/options/yields-spiked-equity-vol-stayed-cheap---options-brief---24-september-2026-24092026) — if that gap closes through equity selling rather than rates calming, financial conditions will fall further and take market momentum with them. The view changes on a confirmed Hormuz reopening (Iran tabled a deal tied to nuclear inspector access, but nothing resolved by September 28), on Treasury yields reversing as inflation data softens, or on an explicit Fed signal that hiking is done — without one of those three, the path of least resistance for market momentum remains lower.
