Market momentum barely moved — up from -0.62 to -0.59 over the past day — and the components tell the same story as last week, just with the bond market stress baked further in. The previous brief flagged that the divergence between equity and bond volatility was the key tension to watch; it has not resolved. The [MOVE index has pushed higher still, with the 10-year Treasury yield reaching 5.21–5.23% by late last week](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/26/10-year-treasury-yield-is-at-its-highest-in-19-years-how-we-got-here.html) — its highest in nearly two decades — driven by [University of Michigan consumer inflation expectations jumping to 4.6% in September from 4.0% in August](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4949891-treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026), which pushed markets to price a further Fed rate hike with roughly 66% odds for next month. Financial conditions are -0.27, and while the one-day change is a marginal +0.01, the seven-day reading of -0.63 captures the full weight of that bond market dislocation from earlier in the week; the component has stabilized rather than recovered. The one piece of genuine good news is breadth, which extended its improvement to -0.94 — still negative but up +0.05 on the day and +0.58 over seven days — as [more stocks climbed back above their trend lines](https://tickeron.com/trading-investing-101/sp-500-market-breadth-surge-69-of-stocks-above-50day-ma-signals-bullish-opportunities-for-retail-investors-in-2026/), with roughly 69% of S&P 500 names now trading above their 50-day moving average, even though the rally's headline gains remain concentrated in AI and mega-cap names. Growth impulses at -0.56 are essentially unchanged, with copper holding near $6.70 per pound but the dollar index at 119.51 continuing to act as a weight on commodity demand signals globally, and the yield curve's steepening still reflecting inflation worry rather than the sort of short-rate-led easing that would boost the growth read. High-yield credit spreads at 2.80 percent are historically very tight — in the richest decile — and that is the main reason financial conditions are not worse; [equity credit markets have so far refused to price the same risk that bond volatility is telegraphing](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/25/bond-volatility-surges-while-bitcoin-and-wall-street-stay-calm). The view gets more constructive if the 10-year yield retreats from current levels and MOVE follows it lower, which would allow financial conditions to recover and give market momentum a real path higher; if the Fed actually hikes next month on the back of sticky inflation expectations, yields stay elevated, MOVE stays elevated, and the -0.59 reading is the ceiling rather than the floor.
