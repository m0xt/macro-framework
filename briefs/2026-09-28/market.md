The breadth watch-point from last week's brief resolved in the right direction — improving from roughly -1.52 to -0.94 over seven days (+0.58), with [69% of S&P 500 stocks trading above their 50-day moving averages](https://tickeron.com/trading-investing-101/sp-500-market-breadth-surge-69-of-stocks-above-50day-ma-signals-bullish-opportunities-for-retail-investors-in-2026/) against roughly 30% a week prior — but financial conditions blew up in the opposite direction (-0.63 over seven days, from roughly +0.36 to -0.27), nearly perfectly canceling the breadth gain and leaving market momentum at -0.59 on the week, materially worse than the -0.32 in the previous brief. The driver is the bond market: the MOVE index [surged roughly 19% on the week to 96.00](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/treasury-volatility-set-for-biggest-jump-in-year-as-bonds-churn) — its [biggest single-week jump in over a year](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/u-s-treasury-market-volatility-hits-historical-highs-move-index-surges-19-weekly) — as the 10-year yield rose to [5.18%, its highest level in nearly two decades](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/26/10-year-treasury-yield-is-at-its-highest-in-19-years-how-we-got-here.html), pushed there by a jump in consumer inflation expectations (University of Michigan's survey: 4.6% from 4.0% in August), [worsening fiscal projections from the CBO](https://fortune.com/2026/09/26/us-debt-outlook-cbo-10-year-treasury-bond-yields-highest-two-decades/), and a surge in corporate bond supply from AI-driven tech spending competing with Treasuries for buyers. The most striking signal is the [divergence between bond and equity volatility](https://www.home.saxo/en-ch/content/articles/options/bond-vol-overtakes-equity-vol---options-brief---25-september-2026-25092026): the MOVE is now running above the VIX — rate volatility at 96.00 versus equity volatility at 14.87 — meaning the Treasury market is pricing in genuine policy and inflation stress while stock traders remain relatively calm, a combination that [analysts including Bank of America's Michael Hartnett flag as a deleveraging risk](https://gokhshtein.com/news/2026-09-26-move-index-rises-35-as-hartnett-flags-deleveraging-risk) if the two converge by equities catching down rather than bonds calming down. The breadth improvement is real but needs a caveat: the S&P 500 equal-weight index [closed at a three-month low](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-500-nears-record-breadth-211359123.html) even as the cap-weighted index trades near records, so participation is wider than last week but still skewed toward large-cap AI and tech names. Growth impulses contributed nothing to the week's move (+0.02), with copper and shipping holding broadly where they were — the Hormuz disruption still distorts the shipping signal toward supply stress rather than demand. The view improves if MOVE retreats back below the high-eighties, bond vol and equity vol stop diverging, and the equal-weight index stops lagging — that combination would restore financial conditions and let the breadth improvement compound into something durable; if the 10-year yield pushes toward 5.25–5.30% on an October hike and the MOVE stays elevated, financial conditions stay pinned negative and the breadth improvement is noise.
