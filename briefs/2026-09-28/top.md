The headline allocation posture edged from -0.87 to -0.84 — a marginal improvement that still leaves the framework sitting 1.09 points below the LONG threshold and firmly in CASH. The prior brief's central question — whether the divergence between calm equity markets and surging bond volatility would resolve — got its answer this week: it did not, and the gap widened. The [10-year Treasury yield reached 5.21–5.23%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/26/10-year-treasury-yield-is-at-its-highest-in-19-years-how-we-got-here.html), [University of Michigan inflation expectations jumped from 4.0% to 4.6%](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4949891-treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026), and markets moved to pricing roughly 66% odds of another Fed rate hike — all of which kept bond volatility elevated and financial conditions pinned at -0.27, even as equity credit spreads stayed historically tight; that's the split in a single sentence. Breadth did what the prior brief expected — 69% of S&P 500 names are now trading above their 50-day moving average — but the financial conditions drag absorbed almost all of that gain, which is why market momentum moved only from -0.62 to -0.59. The economy pillar is still running at full strength — the Atlanta Fed's Q3 nowcast holds at 5.0%, jobless claims near 197,000, macro buffer at +0.50, stress at +0.00 — and the prior brief's root cause is now confirmed: the same robust growth that keeps the macro buffer healthy is the force sustaining the higher-for-longer narrative that is keeping bond yields elevated, so the two pillars are locked in a structural tug of war rather than converging. Tomorrow's [August PCE report](https://stockmarkethours.org/events/us-pce-release) is the first real test of whether that tension is building or easing, and a hot core reading would make [October 14 CPI](https://nowflation.com/cpi-release-dates) — where three consecutive 0.3% monthly prints are already stacking up — the most consequential single input this framework has seen in months, because a fourth would start bending the six-month inflation window in a direction that could eventually erode the macro buffer. The posture returns to CAUTION only if bond yields retreat far enough to let financial conditions recover; with inflation expectations rising and a Fed hike now being actively priced, that path is not open yet.
