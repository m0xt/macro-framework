The macro stress score held at +0.00 for the second consecutive week — still firmly Calm — with the macro buffer unchanged at full strength (+0.50), meaning the economy pillar is contributing maximum tailwind to the headline posture. Last week's main open question was whether the labor market would start to crack under four percent rates; [initial claims for the week ending September 19 came in at 197K, beating the 201K consensus](https://investinglive.com/news/us-initial-jobless-claims-197k-vs-201k-expected/) and sitting within a rounding error of the prior week's cycle-low 196K — that question is now closed for the moment. The broader growth picture corroborates: [GDPNow ticked to 5.0% on September 25](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-atlanta-fed-gdpnow-q3-50-prev-51) from 5.1%, with personal consumption actually revised up to 4.2% for the quarter, while a small pullback in business investment offset it — the composition matters because durable consumer spending is a sturdier engine than inventory rebuilds or a capex surge, and the real economy score stayed flat at +0.00 consistent with healthy conditions. The inflation axis is unchanged for a second straight week: the six-month core CPI direction held at -0.10 with the annual rate still at 2.40%, frozen until the [October 14 CPI print](https://nowflation.com/cpi-release-dates), where [market consensus sits at 3.7% for headline and the Cleveland Fed's nowcast runs closer to 3.47%](https://nowflation.com/cpi-release-dates) — a spread that leaves real uncertainty about whether September becomes a fourth consecutive hot monthly reading. The [September 30 PCE report](https://www.bea.gov/data/personal-consumption-expenditures-price-index) is the next live input: if real spending slips and October 14 then delivers another 0.3% or more on core, both axes start moving toward stress simultaneously — which is the only path that threatens the Calm reading and begins to erode the macro buffer.
