The circuit break never came. [The Fed delivered a 25-basis-point hike to 3.75%–4.00%](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/fed-meeting-updates-and-commentary-september-2026) — [the first rate increase since 2023, and what prediction markets had priced at roughly 87% going in](https://robinhood.com/us/en/prediction-markets/economics/events/fed-rate-decision-in-september-2026-sep-16-2026/) — but Chair Warsh's press conference kept the door to further tightening open, and broad market participation kept falling: the breadth component dropped -0.31 on the day to -1.51, its worst level in this downturn, with only roughly a third of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day moving average and the selling now spreading well beyond the semiconductor names that started the week's losses. Market momentum deepened to -0.75, a -0.14 move on the day and -0.58 over seven days, now almost a quarter-point past the CASH threshold and still moving lower. Financial conditions slipped another -0.05 to +0.02 — barely positive and down -0.41 over the past week — as [high-yield credit spreads widened to 2.71 basis points](https://govspending.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2/) from 2.65 and the VIX ticked to 17.20; the 10-year Treasury yield did pull back to 4.97% from Tuesday's 5.04% intraday peak, which helped, but not enough to offset the spread and volatility pressure. Growth impulses edged -0.06 lower to -0.75: [copper recovered partially to around $6.39 per pound](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/copper) after reports that the Trump administration delayed a tariff decision on refined metal, a marginal positive, but [the Strait of Hormuz is still running at roughly six commercial transits per day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis) against a pre-crisis norm near 85, and shipping remains a consistent and unresolved drag on the growth impulse score. The path back toward positive territory now runs entirely through breadth: that component needs a sustained recovery — more stocks holding above their 50-day average — and for that to happen, markets need either a natural exhaustion of selling pressure or a clear signal that the Fed is done hiking, which Warsh's language today did not provide.
