The inflation watch the previous brief identified as decisive has closed without the shock. [July core CPI came in at +0.2% month-over-month and 2.5% year-over-year](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/cpi-inflation-report-july-2026.html) — in line with consensus and a tick below June's 2.6% — so the dashboard absorbed the print with no movement: the inflation direction metric holds at +0.00, the stress score remains at +0.00 (Calm by the published thresholds), and the macro buffer is fully intact at +0.50. The bigger development this week has come from the labor market: [July payrolls fell by 23,000](https://www.wichitaliberty.org/economics/july-2026-jobs-report-payrolls-fall/) against a +83,000 consensus, led by government job cuts and losses in leisure and retail, and that is pressing the real economy score down to the edge of positive territory at +0.04 — even as the GDPNow component holds a healthy +1.79. The stress framework only escalates when both growth and inflation are moving in the wrong direction at the same time: right now inflation is cooling, and while the labor market is softening, the overall growth signal is still fractionally above zero — spending and output data are holding up where the jobs data is not. Both the payrolls miss and the benign CPI have pushed Fed expectations in the same direction: [markets now lean toward a hold in September rather than a hike](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/july-inflation-data-came-expected-155501202.html), removing one of the near-term upside risks to the stress score. What changes the picture materially is a second consecutive month of job losses in August payrolls (due September 4) — that would pull the real economy score below zero for the first time this cycle and begin pushing the stress score toward Watch territory; today's PPI release and Thursday's retail sales report are the nearer-term checks on whether spending is still covering for the labor softness.
