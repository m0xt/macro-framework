The macro stress score sits at **+0.04** — Calm, and effectively anchored near zero for a second consecutive day — while the macro buffer holds at full strength (**+0.50**), meaning the economy pillar continues to give the headline allocation posture its maximum tailwind. The previous brief's open question was whether goods-spending softness and the ADP private payrolls miss would spread into broader weakness; this week's data pushed back on that worry from the activity side. The [August ISM Services report](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/services-pmi-at-55-4-august-2026-ism-services-pmi-report-302868046.html), released September 3, beat at 55.4 versus a 54.2 estimate — business activity hit 61.7, its strongest since November 2022, and new orders rose to 60.9, the highest since February 2023 — confirming that the pullback in retail spending has not crossed into the larger service economy. [Initial jobless claims](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/u-initial-jobless-claims-rise-125759530.html) for the week ending August 29 came in at 206,000, near cycle lows, and [Atlanta Fed GDPNow](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow) held at 4.7% annualized for Q3 after its personal consumption nowcast ticked up to 3.8%, leaving the real economy score at a barely-negative **-0.06** rather than deteriorating. The week's most consequential single data point, though, was ISM prices paid, which [jumped to 72.6](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/united-states-ism-services-prices-paid-726-august-vs-703-202609031400) from 70.3 — the highest since August 2022, with every industry in the survey reporting higher costs driven by tariffs and energy, marking the 111th consecutive month of services price increases. The inflation direction axis sits at **+0.00** because the six-month change in core Consumer Price Index hasn't moved yet and core remains anchored at 2.50% year-over-year, but services cost pressure at this level has historically preceded core re-acceleration by one to two months. The [August CPI on September 11](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) is now a higher-stakes event than it was a week ago: any meaningful lift in core would turn the inflation direction axis positive and begin eroding the macro buffer, while another tame reading would signal that tariff-driven services costs are not yet passing through to consumers and leave the stress score near zero.
