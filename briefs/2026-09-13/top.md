The headline allocation posture held flat at -0.74 — still CASH — with market momentum at -0.48 clinging to its threshold by 0.02 and the [FOMC decision](https://fedratecalc.com/fomc-meeting-schedule/) now three days away. The prior brief called that decision the single binary that resolves the posture, and the most important new data this week supports that framing rather than settling it: [August core Producer Price Index came in at +0.2% month-over-month, below consensus](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/ppi-inflation-report-august-2026.html), providing the first credible evidence that August's above-forecast Consumer Price Index reading was partly an energy-and-diesel artifact rather than durable broad demand pressure. That has nudged hike odds from the 60–70% range immediately after the Consumer Price Index print down to roughly 56%, but Chair Warsh's public statement that inflation has not "meaningfully improved" and the ongoing [Strait of Hormuz disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis) sustaining oil prices mean the outcome is still genuinely uncertain. The economy pillar is the ballast keeping the posture from slipping further — macro buffer at +0.50, stress at +0.07, and [jobless claims at 206,000](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/jobless-claims) confirming that the GDPNow softness is a trade-accounting effect rather than a real employment deterioration. The market pillar has no equivalent cushion: breadth at -1.24 reflects six sessions of accumulated damage that one day's partial stabilization cannot undo, and financial conditions at +0.20 remain the only thing keeping market momentum above -0.50. Wednesday's announcement is the fulcrum — a hold gives breadth and financial conditions room to keep rebuilding toward CAUTION, while a hike paired with hawkish language on energy-driven inflation almost certainly closes the remaining 0.02 of cushion and drives the posture back toward last week's -0.91.
